# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Malaysian workers whose tasks AI is taking over (first persona: an accounts payable clerk at a Penang shared-services centre with a six-month redeployment window), plus advisors and admins who review plans and knowledge changes. They open HalaTuju anxious and short on time, often on a laptop after work, and want three answers: which of my skills still count, where can I realistically go, and what do I do this week.

## Product Purpose
A career map that updates itself as AI changes work. It reads a CV, maps skills to ESCO, shows routes to growing jobs, and reroutes when an approved market change lands. Success: a user picks a goal job, understands the route in under a minute, and knows their next step for this week.

## Brand Personality
Calm, credible, direct. Speaks like a good career advisor: plain words, specific numbers, no hype. The 3D map carries the wonder; the panel carries the facts.

## Anti-references
- Generic AI-generated dashboards: every element in a pastel card, pills and info icons everywhere, all-caps eyebrows, monospace numbers for no reason.
- Consulting jargon ("no-regret") and labels that need a tooltip to make sense.
- Hero-metric tiles that show a number without saying what it means for the user.

References to learn from: Google Maps directions (route options as rows, then turn-by-turn steps), LinkedIn job matches ("you have 6 of 10 skills"), Coursera / Duolingo learning paths (goal, progress, next up), Linear / Stripe (restraint, hierarchy from type and spacing).

## Design Principles
1. Overview on the map, decisions in the panel: the panel always summarises what the map shows.
2. One question per screen: Explore answers "where could I go", My goal answers "what do I do next", My skills answers "what still counts".
3. Plain words first, detail on demand: a label must make sense without its tooltip.
4. Hierarchy through type and spacing, not boxes: a card only when the thing is a selectable object.
5. Show provenance quietly: sample vs real data is always labelled, never shouted.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA contrast; English and Malay (all copy through i18n); full keyboard use; reduced-motion alternatives for every animation; never rely on colour alone (AI-risk states also carry text).
