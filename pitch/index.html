The problem

Will AI take my job? How much of it? Which of my skills?

Priya has been an accounts payable clerk for 11 years. Next year AI takes over invoice matching. She has six months to move, and nobody can tell her which of her skills still count.

Appendix: how well it works

Three tests that could have embarrassed us.

We ran them anyway, and we show the results as they came out.

01

Fairness test

We gave the same CV six different names, genders, ages, races, religions and marital statuses. The AI received exactly the same text every time, so the skills, matches and routes came out the same.

tests/fairness.test.ts

02

Tamper test

Text hidden in a web page cannot change what the AI reads from a CV, invent data, or skip our checks. Page text is treated as data, never as instructions.

tests/injection.test.ts

03

Live run, 23 Oct 2026

Our agents read 769 articles in 5.5 minutes and sent 7 findings to a person. Every change is saved as a version and can be undone.

Measured on the live server

Appendix: what we say openly

What this prototype cannot do yet.

Sample data in places

The 16-job catalogue in the demo uses labelled sample data. “Check any job” uses the real official list. Most study times are estimates, and our task evidence comes from US data.

Matching is still rough

A nurse matches her own job at only about 30%, because the official list has about 60 skills per job and a CV names about 16. The questions reach about 70% accuracy for trade jobs.

Tests still to run

We have not yet tested clues such as school name or graduation year. The 1,000 futures test a plan; they do not predict any one person’s future.
