The problem

Will AI take my job? How much of it? Which of my skills?

Take Priya, an accounts payable clerk in Penang for 11 years. Her employer is handing invoice matching to AI next year. Staff who can move into a new role within six months stay; the rest are let go. Nobody tells her which of her skills still count, and the courses she finds are out of date before she finishes them.

Today’s tools, such as MyMahir, MYFutureJobs and LinkedIn Career Explorer, compare your skills or suggest courses. None of them gives you a dated, step-by-step plan that updates itself when AI changes your work.

What we built

Three things most tools don’t do.

Not a chatbot and not a dashboard. HalaTuju is a career map: your skills, the jobs they lead to, and a route between them that updates itself when the news changes.

How plans stay current

Agents read the news. Three agents vote. Plain code does the maths.

AI reads text and evidence. Fixed formulas work out every number on screen, so every number can be explained. Every change is saved as a version and can be undone.

  1. Read

    Every 24 hours, our agents read news and research from 74 trusted websites, and must copy the exact quote and link.

  2. Check

    The quote must appear word for word on the page. No source, a quote that is not there, or a duplicate: thrown out.

  3. Vote

    Three agents vote. One checks the evidence is real, recent and trusted. One checks it is about this skill. One is a sceptic that looks for hype or a sales pitch.

  4. Decide

    2 or 3 yes votes: applied, labelled, and it can be undone. 1 yes: a person decides. 0 yes: rejected and recorded.

  5. You choose

    Anyone whose saved plan is affected sees the old route and the new one, and chooses: keep my route, or switch.

How it is built

Six parts, each with one job. 533 tests on every update.

Deployed on Replit, with the whole journey running live on one pipeline. AI reads text; plain code works out every number. If Gemini goes down, CV reading falls back to keyword matching. If RunPod is slow to start, a local model takes over. If no course exists, the screen says “no course listed yet”.

Impact and feasibility

Free for workers, paid by employers.

Retraining one worker costs RM 150. Retrenching one costs RM 22,800 in severance under Malaysian law for a clerk with 11 years’ service. A worker gets a first plan about 15 seconds after uploading a CV. Running cost: RM 0.03 to read a CV and RM 1.05 for a news check. Personal data is removed before any AI call and never stored as a file, in line with PDPA.

Appendix: how well it works

Three tests that could have embarrassed us.

We ran them anyway, and we show the results as they came out.

01

Fairness test

We gave the same CV six different names, genders, ages, races, religions and marital statuses. The AI received exactly the same text every time, so the skills, matches and routes came out the same.

tests/fairness.test.ts

02

Tamper test

Text hidden in a web page cannot change what the AI reads from a CV, invent data, or skip our checks. Page text is treated as data, never as instructions.

tests/injection.test.ts

03

Live run, 23 Oct 2026

Our agents read 769 articles in 5.5 minutes and sent 7 findings to a person. Every change is saved as a version and can be undone.

Measured on the live server

Appendix: what we say openly

What this prototype cannot do yet.

Sample data in places

The 16-job catalogue in the demo uses labelled sample data. “Check any job” uses the real official list. Most study times are estimates, and our task evidence comes from US data.

Matching is still rough

A nurse matches her own job at only about 30%, because the official list has about 60 skills per job and a CV names about 16. The questions reach about 70% accuracy for trade jobs.

Tests still to run

We have not yet tested clues such as school name or graduation year. The 1,000 futures test a plan; they do not predict any one person’s future.
